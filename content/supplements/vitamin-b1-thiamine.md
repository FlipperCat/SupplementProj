## What Is Vitamin B1?

Vitamin B1 (thiamine) is a water-soluble B vitamin that serves as a critical coenzyme in carbohydrate metabolism, energy production, and nervous system function. Unlike most vitamins, thiamine cannot be synthesized by the human body and has no significant storage capacity — daily intake is essential.

**Biochemistry**: Thiamine is converted to thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP), the active coenzyme form. TPP is required by three key enzymes: pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, and transketolase. These enzymes occupy central positions in glucose metabolism and the pentose phosphate pathway (critical for NADPH production and biosynthesis).

Thiamine also plays roles in myelin formation, neurotransmitter synthesis, and mitochondrial ATP production through the citric acid cycle.

## Benefits

### Primary Benefits

- **ATP Production**: Essential cofactor in mitochondrial energy metabolism
- **Glucose Metabolism**: Required for pyruvate dehydrogenase, converting pyruvate to acetyl-CoA
- **Neurological Function**: Supports myelin formation and neurotransmitter synthesis
- **NADPH Production**: Via transketolase in pentose phosphate pathway
- **Cognitive Function**: Supports acetylcholine synthesis and neural transmission

### Secondary Benefits

- Supports cardiovascular function and heart health
- Aids in stress response and mood regulation
- Supports immune function (increased requirement during infection)
- Enhances blood sugar regulation
- Supports peripheral nerve function
- May reduce oxidative stress through NADPH generation

## Mechanism of Action

### The Three Major Metabolic Roles

**1. Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex (PDC)**
- Converts pyruvate (end product of glycolysis) to acetyl-CoA
- Acetyl-CoA enters citric acid cycle for full energy extraction
- Thiamine deficiency blocks this critical step, causing lactate accumulation and reduced ATP

**2. Alpha-Ketoglutarate Dehydrogenase**
- Part of the citric acid cycle
- Converts alpha-ketoglutarate to succinyl-CoA
- Essential for complete oxidation of glucose

**3. Transketolase (Pentose Phosphate Pathway)**
- Generates NADPH (required for biosynthesis and antioxidant defense)
- Produces ribose-5-phosphate (nucleotide synthesis)
- Critical for maintaining cellular redox balance

### Neurological Mechanisms

- **Neurotransmitter synthesis**: Required for acetylcholine production
- **Myelin maintenance**: Essential component of myelin sheath formation
- **Axonal protection**: Supports nerve conduction velocity
- **Antioxidant defense**: NADPH generation supports glutathione regeneration

| **Energy optimization** | 50-100 mg | Ongoing | Biohacker/athlete protocols |

**Note**: Thiamine is water-soluble and continuously excreted. Daily intake is required. Unlike fat-soluble vitamins, toxicity from supplementation is virtually unknown.

### Benfotiamine vs Standard Thiamine

Benfotiamine is a proprietary form with a lipophilic (fat-soluble) structure:
- **30% higher plasma levels** than thiamine HCl
- **Better oral absorption** with higher bioavailability
- **Crosses blood-brain barrier** more efficiently
- **Preferred for neuropathy** — accumulates in nervous tissue
- **More expensive** — typically 3-5x cost of standard thiamine

For biohacking protocols targeting neurological benefits, benfotiamine is superior.

## Timing Optimization

- **With meals**: Fat-soluble derivative (benfotiamine) absorption enhanced with meals
- **Morning**: Thiamine supports energy metabolism; take with breakfast
- **Split dosing**: For doses >100 mg, split into 2 doses to maintain steady-state
- **Consistency**: Daily intake matters more than occasional larger doses (no storage)
- **With B-complex**: Synergistic with other B vitamins

## Deficiency Signs and Testing

### Clinical Deficiency (Beriberi)

**Wet beriberi** (cardiovascular):
- Cardiomyopathy, congestive heart failure
- Edema, fluid retention
- Rapid heart rate
- Dyspnea

**Dry beriberi** (neurological):
- Peripheral neuropathy (symmetrical, lower extremities first)
- Wertnicke-Korsakoff syndrome (ataxia, ophthalmoplegia, confusion)
- Myelin degeneration
- Irreversible if prolonged

**Subclinical deficiency** (more common in biohacking context):
- Reduced energy, fatigue
- Brain fog, poor concentration
- Mood disturbance, irritability
- Muscle weakness
- Reduced exercise tolerance

### Testing

- **RBC transketolase activity assay**: Gold standard (activity increases when thiamine supplemented)
- **Plasma thiamine**: Direct measurement (normal >40 nmol/L)
- **TPP effect test**: Measures enzyme activity before/after thiamine; >25% increase indicates deficiency
- **Functional testing**: More sensitive than absolute levels

**Advanced**: Response to supplementation is diagnostic — rapid improvement in energy/cognition suggests deficiency.

## Stacking Considerations

### Optimal B-Complex Stack

Thiamine works synergistically within B vitamins:

**B-Complex ratios** (for biohacking):
- B1 (Thiamine): 50-100 mg
- B2 (Riboflavin): 25-50 mg
- B3 (Niacin): 50-100 mg
- B5 (Pantothenic acid): 200-500 mg
- B6 (Pyridoxine): 25-100 mg
- B7 (Biotin): 100-500 mcg
- B9 (Folate): 400-800 mcg
- B12 (Cobalamin): 500-2000 mcg

**Synergistic partners for energy**:
- Magnesium (required for ATP synthesis)
- Alpha-lipoic acid (regenerates antioxidants, supports PDC)
- Acetyl-L-carnitine (transports acetyl groups; works downstream of thiamine)
- CoQ10 (electron transport chain; works with thiamine in oxidative metabolism)

### Competition and Absorption

B vitamins compete for absorption in intestines. Taking as balanced complex is preferable to isolated thiamine in high doses.

## Deficiency Risk Factors

### High Risk Groups

- **Chronic alcoholics**: Ethanol reduces thiamine absorption and increases metabolism
- **Dialysis patients**: Thiamine is water-soluble, removed during dialysis
- **Those with hyperemesis**: Severe vomiting or malabsorption syndromes
- **Hypermetabolic states**: Hyperthyroidism, fever, intense exercise increase requirements
- **Polished rice diets**: Traditional white rice lacks thiamine (refined grains)
- **Older adults**: Reduced absorption, increased metabolism

### Increased Requirements

- **Intense exercise**: Increased glucose oxidation; up to 5x normal requirement
- **High-carbohydrate diets**: More glucose to metabolize requires more thiamine
- **Stress (physical/mental)**: Increases metabolic rate and urinary losses
- **Fever/infection**: Metabolic rate increases; immune cells need thiamine
- **Diabetes**: Hyperglycemia increases glucose oxidation needs

## Research Summary

### Energy Metabolism
- **Glucose oxidation**: Thiamine is rate-limiting for PDC; deficiency reduces ATP by 40-60%
- **Athletic performance**: Supplementation improves endurance in thiamine-depleted athletes
- **Metabolic flexibility**: Supports mitochondrial function in both glucose and fat oxidation

### Neurological Health
- **Neuropathy**: Benfotiamine shows superior efficacy to thiamine HCl in multiple studies
- **Cognitive function**: Mixed evidence; benefits clearer in deficient individuals
- **Mood**: Some evidence for anxiety/depression reduction, particularly in deficient populations
- **Alcohol-induced damage**: Thiamine mitigates Wernicke-Korsakoff development in alcoholics

### Cardiovascular
- **Heart function**: Supports cardiomyocyte ATP production
- **Thiamine-responsive megaloblastic anemia (TRMA)**: Rare genetic condition responsive to high-dose thiamine

### Emerging Research
- **Diabetic complications**: Benfotiamine may reduce advanced glycation end products (AGEs)
- **Cancer**: Limited evidence; some chemotherapy protocols use it prophylactically
- **Cognitive aging**: Potential role in maintaining mitochondrial function with age

## Deficiency vs. Suboptimal Status

**Critical distinction for biohackers**:

Clinical deficiency (beriberi) is rare in developed countries but **suboptimal thiamine status is common**. You may not have deficiency but could have suboptimal:
- Energy production
- Glucose handling
- Neurological reserve
- Cognitive performance

The RDA (1-1.2 mg) prevents deficiency but may be suboptimal for:
- Athletes with high carbohydrate oxidation
- Those targeting cognitive performance
- Aging individuals
- Those under chronic stress

This is why biohackers often supplement at 25-100 mg — several times the RDA.

## Bottom Line

Vitamin B1 (thiamine) is a foundational biohacking supplement for energy metabolism and neurological function. While frank deficiency is uncommon, suboptimal status is widespread and impairs both physical and cognitive performance.

**Key takeaways**:

- **Baseline recommendation**: 50 mg daily (order of magnitude above RDA)
- **Energy optimization**: Consider 100 mg split between breakfast and lunch
- **Neuropathy/nerve support**: Prefer benfotiamine 100-300 mg daily
- **With meals**: Enhanced absorption, especially for fat-soluble forms
- **As part of B-complex**: Synergistic with other B vitamins
- **High margin of safety**: No toxicity concerns even at 500+ mg daily
- **Best for**: Athletes, high-carb dieters, cognitive performers, those with energy complaints

Thiamine is particularly valuable for biohackers because it directly influences the most fundamental biological process: ATP production. The difference between adequate and optimal thiamine status can be substantial for energy, endurance, and mental clarity.
