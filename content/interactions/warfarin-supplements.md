## Overview

Warfarin (Coumadin) is an anticoagulant (blood thinner) prescribed to prevent blood clots, stroke, and pulmonary embolism. It works by inhibiting vitamin K-dependent clotting factors (II, VII, IX, and X). Warfarin has a narrow therapeutic index — the difference between an effective dose and a dangerous dose is small — and its effectiveness is measured by the INR (International Normalized Ratio).

Warfarin is one of the most interaction-prone medications in existence. Many supplements affect either its metabolism or its mechanism of action. Any supplement that affects blood clotting, vitamin K levels, or liver enzyme activity can destabilize INR and create dangerous situations. The cardinal rule: consult your doctor before taking ANY new supplement.

## Safe & Beneficial Supplements

### Vitamin D3
Does not affect blood clotting or warfarin metabolism. Particularly important for warfarin users because long-term anticoagulation is associated with reduced bone density. Vitamin D3 (2,000-4,000 IU) supports calcium absorption and bone health.

### Probiotics
Generally safe with warfarin at standard doses. Supports gut health and does not significantly affect INR levels. Choose standard multi-strain probiotics and avoid mega-dose formulas.

### Vitamin C (Moderate Dose)
Safe at moderate doses up to 500 mg daily. Supports immune function and vascular integrity. Very high doses (over 1,000 mg) may theoretically reduce warfarin effectiveness, so keep doses moderate.

### B-Complex
Safe with warfarin and supports homocysteine metabolism — important because elevated homocysteine is a clot risk factor. B vitamins do not affect clotting factors or INR levels at standard supplemental doses.

## Use With Caution

### Omega-3 Fish Oil
Low-dose fish oil (1-2 grams daily) is generally safe and provides cardiovascular benefits. However, omega-3s have mild antiplatelet effects, and high doses (above 3 grams) may increase bleeding risk. Always inform your anticoagulation clinic when starting fish oil and have your INR checked within 1-2 weeks.

### Magnesium
Generally safe but can affect warfarin absorption if taken simultaneously. Separate magnesium supplementation from warfarin by at least 2 hours. Inform your anticoagulation clinic about any new supplements.

### CoQ10
CoQ10 is structurally similar to vitamin K2 and has occasionally been reported to reduce warfarin's effectiveness. The clinical significance is debated, but if you start CoQ10, have your INR closely monitored for the first month.

## Supplements to Avoid

### Vitamin K Supplements
Vitamin K is warfarin's direct antagonist — it activates the very clotting factors warfarin suppresses. Taking vitamin K supplements will counteract your medication and can cause dangerous blood clots. Even inconsistent vitamin K dietary intake destabilizes INR. Maintain a steady diet and never take vitamin K supplements.

### Garlic Supplements
Concentrated garlic supplements contain allicin and ajoene, which inhibit platelet aggregation through multiple mechanisms. This compounds warfarin's anticoagulant effect, significantly increasing the risk of major bleeding events including GI hemorrhage and intracranial bleeding.

### Ginkgo Biloba
Ginkgo is a potent inhibitor of platelet-activating factor (PAF). When combined with warfarin, the dual anticoagulant and antiplatelet effects dramatically increase bleeding risk. Published case reports include subdural hematoma and spontaneous bleeding in patients combining ginkgo with warfarin.

### High-Dose Ginger
At high supplemental doses, ginger inhibits thromboxane synthetase, enhancing the anticoagulant effect of warfarin. While culinary amounts of ginger are generally safe, concentrated ginger supplements should be avoided.

## Timing Recommendations

Take warfarin at the same time every day as prescribed. Any new supplement should be discussed with your anticoagulation clinic BEFORE starting. Have your INR checked within 1-2 weeks of starting any new supplement. Maintain consistent vitamin K dietary intake — do not suddenly increase or decrease green vegetable consumption.

## When to Talk to Your Doctor

Consult your anticoagulation clinic before starting ANY supplement — this is not optional with warfarin. Seek immediate medical attention for signs of bleeding: unusual bruising, blood in urine or stool, bleeding gums, prolonged bleeding from cuts, severe headache, or dizziness. Also report signs of clotting: leg swelling, chest pain, or sudden shortness of breath.
