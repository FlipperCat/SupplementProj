## Overview

Thyroid medications — including levothyroxine (Synthroid, Levoxyl), liothyronine (Cytomel), and natural desiccated thyroid (Armour Thyroid) — replace or supplement thyroid hormones in patients with hypothyroidism. These medications have a narrow therapeutic window and are highly sensitive to interactions with supplements, particularly minerals.

The single most important rule for thyroid medication users is timing: thyroid hormones must be taken on an empty stomach, and mineral supplements must be separated by at least 4 hours. Many patients have unstable thyroid levels simply because they take supplements too close to their medication.

## Safe & Beneficial Supplements

### Selenium
Selenium is the most important supplement for thyroid health. It is a critical cofactor for deiodinase enzymes that convert inactive T4 (what levothyroxine provides) into active T3. Studies show selenium supplementation (200 mcg daily) reduces thyroid peroxidase (TPO) antibodies in Hashimoto's patients by up to 40%, slowing autoimmune thyroid destruction.

### Zinc
Zinc is required for TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) production and T3 receptor binding. Zinc deficiency impairs thyroid function even when medication doses are adequate. Take 15-30 mg daily, at least 4 hours after thyroid medication.

### Vitamin D3
Hashimoto's thyroiditis patients have significantly higher rates of vitamin D deficiency compared to the general population. Vitamin D supports immune regulation and may help modulate the autoimmune attack on the thyroid. Take 2,000-5,000 IU daily with a fat-containing meal.

### B-Complex
Supports energy production, which is often impaired in hypothyroidism. B12 deficiency is particularly common in autoimmune thyroid disease (Hashimoto's), which often coexists with pernicious anemia. A methylated B-complex addresses these needs.

### Omega-3 Fish Oil
Reduces the chronic inflammation associated with autoimmune thyroid conditions. Supports brain function and cardiovascular health, both of which can be compromised in hypothyroidism. Take 1,000-2,000 mg EPA+DHA daily.

## Use With Caution

### Iodine
Iodine is essential for thyroid hormone synthesis, but the relationship is complex. In Hashimoto's disease (the most common cause of hypothyroidism), excess iodine can accelerate thyroid destruction and worsen the condition. Only supplement iodine if testing confirms deficiency, and use moderate doses (150-300 mcg) under endocrinologist supervision.

### Ashwagandha
Ashwagandha may stimulate thyroid hormone production by increasing T4 and T3 levels. While this sounds beneficial, it can cause hyperthyroid symptoms (racing heart, anxiety, weight loss) in patients already receiving thyroid hormone replacement. Requires careful dose monitoring and medical supervision.

## Supplements to Avoid (Within 4 Hours)

### Calcium
Calcium forms insoluble complexes with thyroid hormones in the GI tract, reducing absorption by up to 60%. This is one of the most common causes of unstable thyroid levels. Calcium supplements, calcium-fortified foods, and antacids containing calcium must all be separated by at least 4 hours from thyroid medication.

### Iron
Iron chelates thyroid hormones, reducing bioavailability by up to 75%. Even small amounts of iron can significantly impair thyroid medication absorption. Take iron supplements at least 4 hours after thyroid medication. Consider iron bisglycinate for better tolerance.

### Magnesium
While beneficial for thyroid patients, magnesium hydroxide and magnesium citrate interfere with thyroid hormone absorption. Take magnesium at least 4 hours after your thyroid dose — evening dosing works well since thyroid medication is typically taken in the morning.

## Timing Recommendations

Take thyroid medication first thing in the morning on an empty stomach with a full glass of water. Wait at least 30-60 minutes before eating breakfast. Wait at least 4 hours before taking any mineral supplement (calcium, iron, magnesium, zinc). Selenium and vitamin D can be taken with lunch. Minerals are best taken with dinner or at bedtime.

## When to Talk to Your Doctor

Always consult your endocrinologist before adding supplements. Get thyroid levels (TSH, free T4, free T3) checked 6-8 weeks after starting any new supplement that affects thyroid function. Report symptoms of hyperthyroidism (racing heart, anxiety, weight loss, tremor) or worsening hypothyroidism (fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance).
