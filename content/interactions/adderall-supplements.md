## Overview

Adderall (mixed amphetamine salts) and similar ADHD stimulant medications (Vyvanse, Ritalin, Concerta, Dexedrine) work by increasing dopamine and norepinephrine availability in the brain. While effective for attention and focus, these medications increase nutrient demands, particularly for magnesium, B vitamins, and zinc.

Common Adderall side effects — insomnia, appetite suppression, jaw clenching, anxiety, and crashes — can be significantly mitigated through strategic supplementation. Understanding which supplements support and which interfere with stimulant medications is essential for ADHD patients.

## Safe & Beneficial Supplements

### Magnesium Glycinate
The most important supplement for Adderall users. Amphetamines increase magnesium excretion through the kidneys while simultaneously increasing neuromuscular excitability. This depletion manifests as muscle tension, jaw clenching (bruxism), restless legs, and insomnia. Magnesium glycinate (200-400 mg at bedtime) specifically addresses these effects and promotes sleep quality.

### Omega-3 Fish Oil
Multiple meta-analyses show omega-3 supplementation independently improves ADHD symptoms (attention, hyperactivity, impulsivity). When combined with stimulant medication, omega-3s may enhance therapeutic response. EPA-dominant formulas (1,000-2,000 mg EPA+DHA) are most studied for ADHD.

### L-Theanine
Found naturally in green tea, L-theanine increases alpha brain wave activity, promoting calm, focused attention. It complements Adderall beautifully by smoothing the stimulant curve — reducing the jittery peak and the afternoon crash. Take 100-200 mg in the morning alongside or shortly after Adderall.

### B-Complex (Methylated)
Amphetamines increase B vitamin utilization for dopamine and norepinephrine synthesis. Adderall essentially burns through B vitamins faster. A methylated B-complex ensures the cofactors needed for neurotransmitter production remain available throughout the day.

### Vitamin D3
ADHD populations have significantly lower vitamin D levels than controls. Supplementing with 2,000-4,000 IU daily supports cognitive function, mood regulation, and immune health.

### Zinc
Zinc is a critical cofactor in dopamine synthesis, transport, and receptor binding. Children with ADHD have consistently lower zinc levels. Studies show zinc supplementation (15-30 mg) improves attention and may reduce the stimulant dose needed for adequate symptom control.

## Use With Caution

### Vitamin C (Timing Critical)
Vitamin C acidifies urine, which dramatically increases renal excretion of amphetamines. This can cut Adderall's effective duration by hours. However, vitamin C is otherwise healthy and beneficial. The solution: take vitamin C only in the evening (6+ hours after Adderall) so it does not reduce daytime medication effectiveness.

### Melatonin
Adderall-related insomnia is extremely common. Low-dose melatonin (0.5-3 mg) at bedtime can help restore sleep onset. Start with the lowest effective dose, as stimulant medications may alter melatonin sensitivity. Extended-release melatonin formulas are often more effective for stimulant-related insomnia.

### Iron
Low ferritin (iron storage) is consistently linked to worse ADHD symptoms and reduced stimulant response. Testing ferritin levels is recommended — target above 30 ng/mL, ideally above 50 ng/mL. However, do not supplement iron without testing, as excess iron causes oxidative damage. Iron bisglycinate is the gentlest form.

## Supplements to Avoid

### Caffeine Supplements
Combining concentrated caffeine supplements with Adderall stacks stimulant effects on the cardiovascular and nervous systems. This increases heart rate, blood pressure, anxiety, and risk of cardiac events including palpitations and arrhythmias. Small amounts of coffee may be tolerable for some, but avoid caffeine pills, pre-workout supplements, and energy drinks.

### Ginseng
Ginseng has stimulant properties that compound Adderall's cardiovascular effects. Panax ginseng in particular increases norepinephrine and can cause hypertension and tachycardia when combined with amphetamines.

### Yohimbe
A potent alpha-2 adrenergic antagonist that increases norepinephrine release. Combined with Adderall's norepinephrine-boosting effects, yohimbe creates dangerous cardiovascular strain including severe hypertension, anxiety, and cardiac arrhythmias.

## Timing Recommendations

Take Adderall as prescribed, typically in the morning. L-theanine and B-complex can be taken alongside or shortly after Adderall. Take vitamin C, zinc, and iron only in the evening — vitamin C due to pH effects on excretion, and minerals to avoid any absorption interactions. Magnesium glycinate at bedtime helps with sleep and muscle recovery. Avoid all stimulating supplements after noon.

## When to Talk to Your Doctor

Always consult your prescriber before adding supplements to your ADHD medication regimen. Report persistent insomnia, significant appetite loss, chest pain, rapid heartbeat, or severe anxiety. Discuss zinc and iron testing to optimize your supplement strategy. If you experience a significant change in Adderall effectiveness, review your supplement timing (especially vitamin C intake).
