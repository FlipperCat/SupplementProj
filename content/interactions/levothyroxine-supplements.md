## Overview

Levothyroxine (brand names: Synthroid, Levoxyl, Tirosint, Euthyrox) is the most prescribed medication in the United States, used by over 20 million Americans for hypothyroidism. It provides synthetic T4 (thyroxine), which the body converts to active T3 (triiodothyronine) using selenium-dependent deiodinase enzymes.

Levothyroxine has notoriously poor and variable absorption — only 40-80% of the dose reaches the bloodstream under ideal conditions. Minerals, food, coffee, and supplements can further reduce absorption, making strict timing rules essential for stable thyroid levels. Many patients with "resistant" hypothyroidism are simply taking their supplements too close to their medication.

## Safe & Beneficial Supplements

### Selenium
The single most valuable supplement for levothyroxine users. Selenium is the essential cofactor for the type 1, 2, and 3 deiodinase enzymes that convert T4 to active T3. Without adequate selenium, levothyroxine cannot be effectively activated in the body. Studies show 200 mcg daily reduces thyroid antibodies by up to 40% in Hashimoto's patients and improves T4-to-T3 conversion. Selenium does not interfere with levothyroxine absorption.

### Zinc
Supports TSH secretion from the pituitary and T3 receptor binding in target tissues. Zinc deficiency impairs thyroid function even when levothyroxine doses are adequate. Take 15-30 mg daily, but separate from levothyroxine by at least 4 hours.

### Vitamin D3
Hypothyroid patients — particularly those with Hashimoto's thyroiditis — have dramatically higher rates of vitamin D deficiency. Studies show an inverse relationship between vitamin D levels and thyroid antibodies. Supplementing with 2,000-5,000 IU daily supports immune regulation and bone health.

### Vitamin B12
Autoimmune thyroid disease frequently coexists with pernicious anemia and B12 deficiency. Symptoms overlap (fatigue, brain fog, numbness), so deficiency may be missed. Sublingual methylcobalamin (1,000 mcg) absorbs without GI dependence and does not affect levothyroxine.

### Omega-3 Fish Oil
Reduces inflammation associated with autoimmune thyroiditis. Supports cardiovascular health and brain function, both commonly compromised in hypothyroidism. Does not affect levothyroxine absorption at any timing.

## Use With Caution

### Iodine
Iodine is needed to synthesize thyroid hormones, but the thyroid-iodine relationship follows a U-curve: both deficiency and excess cause problems. In Hashimoto's disease, excess iodine accelerates thyroid destruction. Only supplement if urinary iodine testing confirms deficiency, and use conservative doses (150-300 mcg) under endocrinologist supervision.

### Ashwagandha
Clinical studies show ashwagandha can increase TSH, T4, and T3 levels. In patients already on levothyroxine, this could cause hyperthyroid symptoms (palpitations, anxiety, weight loss, tremor). If considering ashwagandha, start at low doses and have thyroid levels rechecked in 6-8 weeks.

### L-Tyrosine
An amino acid precursor to thyroid hormones. Supplementation could theoretically increase thyroid hormone production on top of levothyroxine replacement, leading to excess. Use only under medical supervision with thyroid monitoring.

## Supplements to Avoid (Within 4 Hours)

### Calcium
Calcium forms insoluble precipitates with levothyroxine in the GI tract. Studies show calcium carbonate reduces levothyroxine absorption by up to 60%. This includes calcium supplements, calcium-fortified orange juice, and antacids (Tums). Must be separated by a minimum of 4 hours.

### Iron
Iron chelates levothyroxine, reducing bioavailability by up to 75%. Iron bisglycinate, ferrous sulfate, and even iron-fortified foods can significantly impair absorption. Take iron at dinner or bedtime — at least 4 hours after your morning levothyroxine.

### Magnesium
Magnesium hydroxide, citrate, and oxide all interfere with levothyroxine absorption by forming insoluble complexes. Magnesium is beneficial for hypothyroid patients but must be taken at least 4 hours after levothyroxine. Evening dosing is ideal.

### Soy Isoflavones
Concentrated soy supplements contain genistein and daidzein, which inhibit thyroid peroxidase (the enzyme levothyroxine's mechanism depends on) and may reduce GI absorption. Avoid soy protein isolate supplements and isoflavone capsules. Moderate whole soy foods are acceptable if consistently consumed and separated by 4 hours.

## Timing Recommendations

Take levothyroxine first thing in the morning on a completely empty stomach with a full glass of plain water. Wait at least 30-60 minutes before eating, drinking coffee, or taking any other supplement. Schedule all mineral supplements (calcium, iron, magnesium, zinc) for lunch, dinner, or bedtime — at least 4 hours after your morning levothyroxine dose. Selenium, vitamin D, B12, and omega-3 can be taken with lunch without concern.

## When to Talk to Your Doctor

Always inform your endocrinologist about all supplements you take. Have thyroid levels (TSH, free T4, free T3) rechecked 6-8 weeks after starting any supplement that affects thyroid function (selenium, iodine, ashwagandha). Report symptoms of hyperthyroidism (racing heart, anxiety, weight loss) or worsening hypothyroidism (increasing fatigue, weight gain, cold sensitivity).
