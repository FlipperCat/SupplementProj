Best Supplements for Anxiety and Stress (That Actually Work)

Evidence-based supplements to calm anxiety, reduce stress, and support nervous system health

Why You’re Anxious (And How Supplements Can Help)

Before reaching for supplements, understand that anxiety usually has a root cause:

  • Chronic stress — Depletes neurotransmitters and drives inflammation
  • Nutrient deficiencies — Magnesium, B6, zinc directly affect anxiety pathways
  • Gut health — 90% of serotonin produced in gut; dysbiosis worsens anxiety
  • Sleep deprivation — Amplifies anxiety and stress hormones
  • Caffeine overuse — Triggers or worsens anxiety
  • Trauma/conditioning — Some anxiety is psychological; supplements help support therapy

The best anxiety supplements address these root causes or support GABA and serotonin pathways — not just sedate you.

Tier 1: Address Deficiencies First

These work dramatically if you’re deficient and reduce baseline anxiety substantially.

Magnesium

Who needs it: Most people (~70% deficient); especially those with anxiety

Why it works: Required for GABA production and nervous system relaxation; magnesium blocks NMDA receptors (excitation)

Dose: 200-400 mg daily (glycinate or threonate best for anxiety)

Timeline: 1-2 weeks for anxiety reduction

Best type: Magnesium glycinate (gentle) or threonate (crosses blood-brain barrier)

Signs you need it: Anxiety, muscle tension, racing thoughts, panic

Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxal-5-Phosphate)

Who needs it: Those with anxiety or poor stress response

Why it works: Essential for neurotransmitter production (serotonin, dopamine, GABA)

Dose: 25-100 mg daily (split into 2 doses if high)

Timeline: 1-2 weeks

Warning: High-dose B6 taken long-term (>100 mg daily) can cause neuropathy; cycle or keep moderate

Zinc

Who needs it: Those with low zinc; common in stress, athletes, vegetarians

Why it works: Regulates glutamate (excitatory) and supports GABA signaling

Dose: 15-30 mg daily

Timeline: 2-4 weeks

Best type: Zinc picolinate or glycinate

Vitamin D3

Who needs it: Most people, especially those with seasonal or chronic anxiety

Why it works: Regulates serotonin and dopamine; deficiency linked to depression and anxiety

Dose: 2,000-5,000 IU daily

Timeline: 4-8 weeks

Get tested: Aim for 40-60 ng/mL for anxiety benefit

Tier 2: Anxiety Support (Even Without Deficiency)

L-Theanine

Why it works:

  • Amino acid that increases GABA (primary calming neurotransmitter)
  • Promotes relaxation without sedation
  • Non-habit forming; no tolerance
  • Works within 30-60 minutes

Dose: 100-200 mg 1-3x daily as needed

Timeline: 30-60 minutes

Best for: Acute anxiety, racing mind, social anxiety, generalized anxiety

Can use: Throughout day; especially morning and evening

L-Glycine

Why it works:

  • Amino acid neurotransmitter with inhibitory (calming) properties
  • Reduces cortisol (stress hormone)
  • Non-habit forming; safe long-term
  • Particularly good for stress-related anxiety

Dose: 2-3 grams 1-2x daily

Timeline: 30 minutes for acute effect; weeks for chronic reduction

Best for: Generalized anxiety, stress, sleep anxiety

Bonus: Also improves sleep and muscle recovery

GABA

Why it works:

  • Primary inhibitory (calming) neurotransmitter
  • Directly activates GABA-A receptors
  • Reduces physical tension from anxiety
  • Non-addictive

Dose: 500-1,000 mg 1-3x daily

Timeline: 30-60 minutes

Best for: Physical anxiety symptoms (racing heart, tension), acute panic

Note: Some evidence it crosses blood-brain barrier (controversial); many still find it effective

Ashwagandha

Why it works:

  • Adaptogenic herb (helps body handle stress)
  • Reduces cortisol and anxiety
  • Non-habit forming; works over time
  • Supports both physical and mental stress response

Dose: 300-600 mg KSM-66 standardized extract daily (morning or split dose)

Timeline: 2-4 weeks for full benefit; some effect within 1 week

Best for: Chronic anxiety, burnout, cortisol dysregulation

Note: Works best taken consistently for weeks, not as-needed

5-HTP

Why it works:

  • Precursor to serotonin (mood regulation)
  • Increases serotonin production naturally
  • Particularly helpful if anxious and depressed
  • Non-habit forming

Dose: 50-100 mg 1-2x daily

Timeline: 1-2 weeks for anxiety reduction

Best for: Anxiety with depression, mood-based anxiety

Warning: Don’t combine with SSRIs without medical guidance (serotonin syndrome risk)

Tier 3: Advanced Anxiety Management

Passionflower

Why it works:

  • Traditional anxiolytic herb with GABA-enhancing properties
  • Reduces racing thoughts and rumination
  • Particularly useful for anticipatory anxiety
  • No habit potential

Dose: 200-400 mg standardized extract (0.3% hyperoside) 1-3x daily

Timeline: 30-60 minutes for acute effect

Best for: Anticipatory anxiety, social anxiety, generalized anxiety

L-DOPA (Mucuna Pruriens)

Why it works:

  • Precursor to dopamine (motivation, confidence, mood)
  • Supports anxiety through dopamine rather than GABA
  • Useful for anxiety with low motivation or depression
  • More stimulating than other options

Dose: 100-200 mg or 5 g mucuna extract daily

Timeline: 30-60 minutes

Best for: Anxiety with low motivation, depression-based anxiety

Note: More activating; take in morning; avoid if prone to overstimulation

Valerian Root

Why it works:

  • Traditional herbal anxiolytic
  • GABA-enhancing properties
  • Reduces physical tension from anxiety
  • Good for chronic use unlike some options

Dose: 300-600 mg standardized extract (0.5-1% valerenic acids) 1-2x daily

Timeline: 45-90 minutes (slower than others)

Best for: Chronic anxiety with tension, evening anxiety

Phosphatidylserine (PS)

Why it works:

  • Reduces cortisol (stress hormone) especially when elevated at night
  • Particularly effective for anxiety with elevated stress hormone
  • Improves HPA axis (stress response system) function
  • No habit formation

Dose: 100-300 mg daily (best taken in evening)

Timeline: 2-4 weeks

Best for: Cortisol-driven anxiety, afternoon/evening anxiety spikes

What About These Popular Options?

Benzodiazepines (Xanax, Ativan, Valium)

Verdict: Effective for acute anxiety but highly addictive Recommendation: Medical use only; supplements preferred for chronic anxiety

Buspirone

Verdict: Prescription anxiolytic; works via serotonin pathways Recommendation: Under medical supervision only

Kava Kava

Verdict: Effective for anxiety but liver toxicity risk Recommendation: Avoid or use only under supervision

Cannabidiol (CBD)

Verdict: Some evidence for anxiety; quality varies wildly Recommendation: Try quality, third-party tested CBD (10-30 mg); effects mixed

Caffeine

Verdict: Worsens anxiety in most people Recommendation: Reduce or eliminate if anxious

Alcohol

Verdict: Temporarily masks anxiety but worsens it long-term Recommendation: Avoid for chronic anxiety management

The Anxiety Stack

Foundation (Everyone with Anxiety)

  • Magnesium glycinate: 200-300 mg (morning and/or evening)
  • Vitamin B6: 25-50 mg (morning)
  • Vitamin D3: 2,000-5,000 IU (morning)
  • Zinc: 15-30 mg (with food)

For Acute Anxiety Episodes

Add as needed:

  • L-Theanine: 100-200 mg
  • L-Glycine: 2-3 grams
  • GABA: 500-750 mg

For Chronic Generalized Anxiety

Add:

  • Ashwagandha: 300-600 mg KSM-66 (morning, consistently)
  • Passionflower: 200-400 mg (if racing thoughts)
  • 5-HTP: 50-100 mg (if mood-related)

For Cortisol-Driven Anxiety

Add:

  • Phosphatidylserine: 100-300 mg (evening)
  • Rhodiola: 200-400 mg (morning; adaptogen)

For Advanced/Resistant Anxiety

Add:

  • Valerian: 300-600 mg (evening)
  • Consider therapy + medical evaluation

Sample Daily Protocol

Morning

  • Vitamin D3: 2,000-5,000 IU with breakfast
  • Vitamin B6: 25-50 mg with food
  • Zinc: 15-30 mg with food
  • Ashwagandha: 300-600 mg KSM-66 (if using; take consistently)
  • Magnesium glycinate: 100-150 mg (optional; some prefer full dose at night)

Midday (As Needed for Anxiety)

  • L-Theanine: 100-200 mg
  • L-Glycine: 2-3 grams
  • GABA: 500-750 mg

Late Afternoon (If Needed)

  • Passionflower: 200-400 mg (if anticipatory anxiety or racing thoughts)
  • Second ashwagandha dose: 150-300 mg (if high-dose formula)

Evening

  • 5-HTP: 50-100 mg with light meal (if using)
  • Phosphatidylserine: 100-300 mg with dinner (if cortisol-driven anxiety)
  • Magnesium glycinate: 150-300 mg (45 min before bed)
  • Valerian: 300-600 mg (if using; 45-90 min before bed)

As-Needed Acute Protocol (During Panic/High Anxiety)

  • L-Theanine: 200 mg
  • L-Glycine: 3 grams
  • GABA: 750 mg
  • Deep breathing, cold water on face (vagus nerve activation)

What NOT to Do

Don’t Ignore Underlying Causes

If anxiety stems from trauma, relationship stress, or major life uncertainty, supplements help but therapy is essential.

Don’t Mix Too Many GABA Modulators

Magnesium + GABA + Valerian + Passionflower at high doses may be too much. Start minimal and add gradually.

Don’t Rely on Supplements Alone

Best results come from combining: supplements + therapy + lifestyle (sleep, exercise, diet, social connection).

Don’t Use Anxiety Supplements to Avoid Necessary Worry

Some anxiety is protective and signals real problems needing action. Supplements shouldn’t prevent necessary problem-solving.

Don’t Supplement Long-Term Without Variation

Rotate herbs (4-6 weeks each) to prevent tolerance and ensure they’re still working.

Don’t Take Everything at Once

If new to supplements, start with magnesium + L-theanine for 2 weeks before adding more.

Don’t Ignore Sleep

Anxiety worsens dramatically with sleep loss. Fix sleep first (with magnesium if needed).

When to See a Doctor

Anxiety needs professional evaluation if:

  • It prevents you from functioning (work, relationships, self-care)
  • It’s new and accompanied by physical symptoms (chest pain, shortness of breath)
  • Supplements don’t help after 4-8 weeks of consistent use
  • You’re having panic attacks or obsessive thoughts
  • You’re having any thoughts of self-harm
  • You suspect medications are contributing (SSRIs, stimulants, etc.)

Bottom Line

Start with the foundation: Magnesium is non-negotiable; B6, D3, and zinc address common deficiencies.

Add acute anxiety relief: L-Theanine works within 30-60 minutes for racing mind. GABA works for physical symptoms.

Address chronic anxiety: Ashwagandha (2-4 weeks) and 5-HTP (if mood-related) provide sustained relief.

Layer in as needed: Passionflower for anticipatory anxiety, valerian for evening anxiety, PS for cortisol.

Combine with lifestyle: Supplements support good sleep, exercise, therapy, and healthy social connection — they don’t replace these.

Key takeaways:

  • Magnesium glycinate is foundational (200-300 mg daily)
  • L-Theanine works in 30 minutes for acute anxiety (100-200 mg)
  • Ashwagandha reduces chronic anxiety over 2-4 weeks
  • Combine magnesium + L-theanine for synergistic effect
  • Therapy + supplements > supplements alone
  • Sleep and exercise rival any supplement for anxiety management
  • Avoid caffeine if anxious
  • Test and optimize: D3, B6, zinc, magnesium
